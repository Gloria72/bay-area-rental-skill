# Preference Profile

## Core User Model

The renter wants a comfortable Bay Area apartment that feels newer, quiet enough for a light sleeper, operationally smooth, and not stressful around parking, packages, maintenance, or security.

Primary values:

- Quiet sleep and low vibration/noise.
- Secure parking and package handling.
- Good natural light, open view, and privacy.
- Newer-feeling finishes and reliable appliances.
- In-unit washer/dryer, dishwasher, A/C, and reasonable storage.
- A building where management is responsive and basic operations are not chaotic.
- A lively downtown can be acceptable if it is like Grove Street / The Hendrix: walkable, restaurant-heavy, and urban, but not dominated by hard noise sources.

## Default Fit Rules

- `1B/1B` is preferred; `>=700 sqft` is the normal minimum.
- Studio can be acceptable at `500-650 sqft` if it is newer, bright, efficient, has in-unit laundry, and the building operations are strong.
- Budget target is usually `<= $3,900`; `$3,800` feels more comfortable. Treat `$4,000+` as high-budget and require a clear reason.
- Mountain View and Sunnyvale are preferred. Santa Clara can work only after strict review screening. North San Jose and Santa Clara new-build clusters need extra skepticism because many have garage/package/security/noise complaints.
- Controlled access is helpful but not enough by itself. Real review history around car break-ins, garage gates, packages, and management response matters more.

## Review Audit Rules

Use official pages for features, not for resident experience. Use recent resident reviews for lived risks.

Read the worst recent reviews before trusting ratings. Aggregate scores can hide severe red flags, and platforms can disagree. A building with `4.5/5` on one site and many recent `1-2 star` complaints elsewhere should be treated as mixed, not safe.

Treat repeated patterns as stronger than one-off complaints. Delete or downgrade if multiple recent reviews mention the same renter-core problem:

- Car smashed, stolen, broken into, or insecure garage.
- Packages/mail stolen or mailroom unreliable.
- Garage gate broken, storage room unsafe, or security cameras ineffective.
- Management ignores, ghosts, blames residents, or fails to resolve complaints.
- Frequent false fire alarms, especially overnight.
- Thin walls, upstairs footsteps, train horns, low-frequency vibration.
- Roaches, bugs, ants, rats, mold, water quality, or unresolved maintenance.
- Parking permit/towing confusion, surprise fees, or key/fob process problems.

Be extra skeptical of:

- Brand-new buildings with short or incentivized review samples.
- Greystar-managed properties: not automatic rejection, but a caution flag when paired with weak operations.
- Beautiful new buildings near highways, light rail, airports, stadium/event areas, or industrial corridors.

## Location And Noise Rules

Acceptable:

- Downtown energy similar to Grove Street / The Hendrix.
- Restaurant and pedestrian background noise when the unit is high enough and not directly over retail/nightlife.
- Walkability, Caltrain convenience, and city feel if the exact unit is shielded.

Hard noise sources to avoid or heavily discount:

- `101`, `237`, `280`, `880`.
- Lawrence Expressway, El Camino, Mathilda, Central Expressway.
- Caltrain, VTA, light rail, especially horn-facing or track-facing units.
- SJC airport flight paths, Moffett/industrial/office corridors.
- Levi's Stadium / Great America event-day traffic and crowd noise.
- Garage gates, garage ramps, trash rooms, loading areas, elevators, mechanical rooms.
- Amenity courtyards, BBQ/fireplace zones, pools/spas, gyms, rooftops, lounges.
- Above retail, direct street access, or low-floor street-facing units.

## Unit Scoring

Use this 100-point scoring model for apartment or exact-unit comparison:

| Category | Points | What To Reward |
|---|---:|---|
| Quiet / sleep safety | 30 | Top/high floor, shielded exposure, no hard noise source, low review noise risk |
| Security / parking / packages / management | 25 | Clean recent reviews, secure garage, reliable package system, responsive management |
| Light / view / privacy | 20 | Open exposure, fewer obstructions, higher floor, not visible from public paths |
| Layout / size / daily function | 15 | `1B >=700 sqft`, usable kitchen/living space, in-unit laundry, storage |
| Location / lifestyle / commute | 5 | Pleasant walkability, parks/trails, grocery/restaurants, reasonable commute |
| Price / incentives / fee clarity | 5 | Fits budget, fees understandable, incentives do not mask bad fundamentals |

If the user's question is only about exact unit placement, weight quiet/light/privacy more heavily and downweight neighborhood.

## Floor And Orientation Heuristics

Top floor is the safest answer when upstairs footsteps appear in reviews. Non-top-floor units should be discounted in buildings with thin-wall or upstairs-noise complaints.

Fourth floor or higher is usually better for privacy, light, and reduced pedestrian/driveway noise. Second floor is often a compromise. First floor is possible only when the unit is not street-facing, not garage-facing, has decent privacy, and has strong light.

Courtyard-facing can be good, but not if the courtyard is an amenity zone. Inner courtyards can amplify voices and activity.

End units are often better than mid-corridor units because they can reduce shared-wall and hallway traffic.

Orientation:

- East / northeast: often best balance for morning light and less overheating.
- North / northwest: softer light, can feel dim or compressed if lower floor or blocked.
- South / west: brighter but can be hotter; west exposure can be harsh.
- Actual obstruction beats compass direction. A lower north-facing unit blocked by another building is worse than a higher east-facing unit with open view.

## Decision Labels

Use these labels consistently:

- `主推`: clean enough to focus energy on, still verify current details.
- `条件保留`: can work only under specific unit conditions.
- `备选`: acceptable if stronger options are unavailable.
- `不推荐`: not aligned with core comfort goals.
- `淘汰`: review/location/unit red flags hit core dislikes.

When the user is in screening mode, do not ask them to contact leasing. They explicitly prefer filtering first unless they request outreach language.
